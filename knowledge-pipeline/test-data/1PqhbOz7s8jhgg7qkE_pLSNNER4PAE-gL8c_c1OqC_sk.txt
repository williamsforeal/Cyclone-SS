Here are the products discussed in the video, ranked for viability for testing/approval:High Viability:
1. Sourdough Starter Kit (Price: $150):
   * Reasoning: The product did almost $10,000 in revenue in one day and does not appear to be branded, suggesting it is a generic kit. The speaker estimated it costs only $15-$20 to produce, allowing for a good profit margin.
2. Table Stand/iPad Stand (similar to Cooper Cases):
   * Reasoning: The specific "Cooper Cases" product is branded, but the speaker believes it is a simple product that can be sourced or closely replicated. The product is selling due to its quality, not just its price. The speaker suggested looking into this product more and checking the Meta Ad Library for sourcing options.
3. Heated Portable Throw/Shawl:
   * Reasoning: This is a concept the speaker is waiting for, not an actual product found in the search. It offers a unique solution (portability) compared to typical heated blankets that must be plugged into a wall. If validation is found, it has high potential because it is new and fresh.
Medium Viability (Niche/Angle Dependent):
1. Car Vanity Mirror:
   * Reasoning: The angle for this product is "Passenger Princess". While the product is solid and was successful organically, the low perceived value makes running ads difficult due to the need for a decent margin. The speaker suggested a nice premium bundle or volume bundle might make it work.
2. Baby Products (e.g., Strollers):
   * Reasoning: The speaker says baby products have potential but must be new, unique, and problem-solving, not a regular baby stroller. For example, a revolutionary stroller that folds, is super compact, has smooth abilities, or an attached diaper bag would have potential.
3. Dryer Vent Cleaner:
   * Reasoning: The concept is good, and it is a problem-solving product, but the example seen appears to be custom branded. Viability depends on finding a generic version and the right bundle.
Low Viability (Branded, Novelty, or Logistically Complex):
1. Eye Massager:
   * Reasoning: Too many students are already running this product, making it "not ideal".
2. Branded Products (K-pop Demon Hunter, Popper Bundle, Neato, Cooper Cases, Candida Cleanse, Moroc Ab Twister Plate, Bondi Boost, etc.):
   * Reasoning: Selling branded products means you become an affiliate, earning a commission (e.g., 20-30%) without owning the brand or having any longevity. Competitors are likely already operating at a high level on these products.
3. Pills/Supplements (e.g., Candida Cleanse, URO Vaginal Probiotic Pills, other pills/vitamins):
   * Reasoning: These are usually high-level brands selling custom, proprietary products. Consumers prefer buying these from trusted brands (like Target or Walmart) rather than a random Shopify site that sources from a factory in China. Furthermore, there are higher risks and liability since these are consumables.
4. Cologne/Perfumes:
   * Reasoning: These fall into the same category as supplements because the formulation is unknown, and the product is likely branded.
5. Wearing Heated Blanket (Robe style):
   * Reasoning: While it is selling, the speaker could not identify what makes this particular product better than a regular heated blanket. The foot holes were not considered a "crazy" revolutionary difference.
6. Fire Humidifier and Aroma Diffuser:
   * Reasoning: This is a novelty product that looks cool but does not genuinely solve a problem. The speaker advised against novelty products as they offer less leverage.
7. Walking Pad:
   * Reasoning: It is a problem-solving product, but it is too large, involves complex technology, and is susceptible to damage during shipment, which creates a pain point for the seller due to returns and chargebacks.
8. Wrist Blood Pressure Monitor:
   * Reasoning: The primary demographic for this product is sensitive, and a misread could be "life or death". The speaker would not trust selling a product like this without being fully aware of its technology and capabilities.
9. Kitchen Toy Set:
   * Reasoning: The revenue was likely inflated because the product was selling at a cheap price on its last day. The angle was not focused on problem-solving (teaching kids) but was more for a "nice toy to have".
10. Hair Products (e.g., extensions):
   * Reasoning: These are "hyper-specific" products, and customers usually know where to get their hair. Ordering online involves high risks of quality issues, incorrect links, or wrong colors/shades.
11. Bathroom Sink Stopper:
   * Reasoning: This is a problem-solving product, but its perceived value is too low. The product selling for $7 would need to be marked up to about $30 to cover ad costs, which the speaker believes is too high for the perceived value.
12. Wristband Alarm (for deaf people):
   * Reasoning: The speaker believes this is a custom-branded product, not a generic one. Also, like the blood pressure monitor, the speaker would stay away from it due to concerns about reliability and the possibility of a "life or death" issue.
13. Stanley Tumbler:
   * Reasoning: This is a branded product and likely not one that can be sourced.
14. Leg Compression:
   * Reasoning: The technology is advanced, which increases the risk of chargebacks and refunds. It has been around for a while, meaning competitors have been running it. Viability would depend on a "hyper-specific angle" that is not currently being run.
